# Debt Intelligence — Institutional Grade
## Definition
Debt is NOT inherently negative.
Debt is:
→ A **capital allocation instrument with a defined cost of capital**
BOCY evaluates debt as:
→ A competing use of capital alongside investments, tax structures, and liquidity
---
# CORE PRINCIPLE
The correct question is NOT:
→ "Should I pay off debt?"
The correct question is:
→ "Does allocating capital to this debt produce a better net outcome than alternative uses?"
---
# ROLE OF DEBT IN THE SYSTEM
Debt introduces:
1. Guaranteed cost (interest rate)
2. Liquidity implications
3. Optionality constraints
4. Risk exposure (rate changes, income sensitivity)
---
# DEBT CLASSIFICATION
## Tier 1: High-Interest Debt
- Credit cards
- Personal loans (>6–8%)
→ Guaranteed negative compounding
---
## Tier 2: Medium-Rate Debt
- Car finance
- Personal loans (~4–6%)
→ Context-dependent optimization
---
## Tier 3: Low-Rate Debt (Strategic Debt)
- Mortgages (~2–4%)
→ Often optimal to maintain, not eliminate
---
# EVALUATION FRAMEWORK
Each debt position must be evaluated using:
---
## 1. Cost of Capital
- Effective interest rate
- Fixed vs variable
- After-tax cost (if applicable)
---
## 2. Alternative Return
- Probability-weighted return (via Monte Carlo)
- Risk-adjusted return (RAR)
---
## 3. Liquidity Impact
- Cash required to reduce debt
- Buffer implications
---
## 4. Time Horizon
- Remaining term
- Compounding effects
---
## 5. Flexibility
- Early repayment penalties
- Re-access to capital (e.g. redraw, refinancing)
---
# DECISION MODEL
## Net Benefit of Repayment
Repayment Utility =
Guaranteed Interest Saved
- Lost Alternative Return
- Liquidity Cost
- Optionality Cost
---
## Net Benefit of Retaining Debt
Retention Utility =
Alternative Return (probability-weighted)
- Interest Cost
- Risk Penalty
---
## Decision Rule
Repay debt IF:
→ Risk-adjusted return of alternative < cost of debt
→ OR liquidity risk is elevated
Retain / optimize debt IF:
→ Probability-weighted return of alternative > cost of debt
AND
→ Downside risk is acceptable
---
# MONTE CARLO INTEGRATION
Debt vs investment decisions MUST use simulation:
Outputs required:
- Probability investment outperforms debt
- Median net gain/loss
- Downside scenario
---
## Example
Debt:
- Mortgage @ 3.2%
Investment:
- Expected 6%, volatile
Monte Carlo Output:
- 68% probability investment > mortgage
- Median gain: £28,000 (10 years)
- Downside: -£9,000
---
## Insight Requirement
BOCY must present:
- Both scenarios
- Probability of success
- Downside exposure
---
# PARTIAL ALLOCATION (CRITICAL)
Avoid binary decisions.
Instead:
→ Split capital across:
- Debt repayment
- Investment
- Liquidity
---
## Example
Instead of:
→ "Pay off mortgage" OR "Invest everything"
BOCY:
→ "Allocate £500/month to mortgage, £500/month to ISA"
---
# LIQUIDITY CONSTRAINT OVERRIDE
Debt repayment MUST NOT:
- Reduce liquidity below threshold
- Create cashflow instability
---
## Rule
Liquidity constraint overrides debt optimization
---
# INTEREST RATE SENSITIVITY
For variable-rate debt:
- Model future rate scenarios
- Stress-test affordability
---
## Insight Trigger
If rate increase materially changes optimal strategy:
→ Generate alert
---
# TAX CONSIDERATIONS
Account for:
- Mortgage tax treatment (if applicable)
- Investment tax wrappers (ISA, pension)
---
## Rule
After-tax comparison is mandatory
---
# BEHAVIOURAL LAYER
Users often:
- Over-prioritize debt elimination (risk aversion)
- Underestimate opportunity cost
BOCY must:
→ Quantify trade-offs clearly
→ Avoid emotional framing
→ Present rational allocation
---
# INSIGHT TYPES (DEBT-SPECIFIC)
1. **Overpayment Inefficiency**
→ Paying low-rate debt instead of investing
2. **Yield Mismatch**
→ Holding low-return assets while paying higher interest
3. **Liquidity Misallocation**
→ Using too much cash to reduce debt
4. **Rate Risk Exposure**
→ Vulnerability to interest rate changes
---
# RECOMMENDATION STANDARD
Every debt-related recommendation must include:
1. Current strategy
2. Alternative strategy
3. Probability-weighted outcome
4. Downside scenario
5. Net financial difference
---
# HARD RULES
- Never assume debt should be eliminated
- Never ignore alternative returns
- Never ignore liquidity constraints
- Never provide binary recommendations without trade-off
---
# SYSTEM OBJECTIVE
BOCY ensures:
→ Debt is used as a **strategic tool**, not a default liability
User must feel:
→ "This is optimizing my debt in a way I would not have considered"
→ "I understand exactly why keeping or repaying this debt is better"
